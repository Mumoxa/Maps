# Top 500 South African company directory import — 15 October 2026

## Objective and current baseline

The brief was to add every company in a Top 500 SA company directory export
(`top500_categorised.csv`) to the Industry Atlas **without duplicating** anything,
and to place each company in its real national-taxonomy branch rather than the
"Not yet classified" queue. Three scope decisions were taken with the requester up
front:

1. **Placement — full.** Map each directory sector label onto the national
   taxonomy and extend the Maps industry/pocket vocabulary and the crosswalk where
   the taxonomy could not represent the label. Not "existing industries only", and
   not "everything into the backlog".
2. **Existing companies — upgrade.** Companies Maps already knows are never
   recreated: the curated record is enriched with the listing as an extra source,
   and a dataset-only stub becomes a curated record with the same identity.
3. **Evidence — listing only.** Each record cites its own directory listing, the
   sector the directory reports and the location it reports, read on
   **2026-10-15**. Nothing is marked `verified`; every record stays
   `needs-verification`.

The export carries 251 rows, **208 distinct companies** and 108 distinct sector
labels. It is stored with the batch configuration under
`markets/organizations/imports/top500-companies-2026-10-15/`:

| File | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `source/top500_categorised.csv` | The export as received. |
| `category-industry-map.json` | Every directory label → Maps industry, plus row-level overrides. |
| `taxonomy-additions.json` | The taxonomy growth the labels required (nodes, industries, pockets, crosswalk bindings). |
| `identity-overlay.json` | Curated identity decisions: merges, name forms, curated records that only take the listing as a source. |
| `import-report.json` | Machine-readable outcome of the import, including the review list of dataset-only employers left as separate organisations. |

## Taxonomy extension

The Atlas vocabulary before this batch was 30 industries in 12 pockets, aimed at
cold chain, food, agriculture, property, construction and renewables. A directory
of national scale cannot be expressed in that vocabulary, so the national taxonomy
was extended first, and the extension is data, not code:

- **10 new taxonomy nodes**: investment management and holding companies
  (`FIN-ASSET`, `FIN-ASSET-MANAGE`, `FIN-ASSET-HOLD`), heavy equipment and
  machinery (`MFG-MACH`, `MFG-MACH-EARTH`, `MFG-MACH-IND`), passenger airlines
  (`TRA-AIR-PAX`), vehicle rental and fleet leasing (`TRA-SVC-RENT`), auctions and
  asset realisation (`PRO-CORP-AUCT`), and B-BBEE verification
  (`PRO-CONSULT-BBBEE`).
- **88 new Maps industries** across 18 new pockets (banking and finance,
  investment and capital, mining and extractives, energy and utilities, commercial
  farming, industrial manufacturing, automotive, consumer retail, technology,
  telecommunications, media and marketing, professional advisory, facilities
  services, health and wellness, hospitality and tourism, aviation and defence,
  public sector, education and skills, food manufacturing).
- Vocabulary after the batch: **118 industries, 30 pockets, 741 taxonomy nodes**,
  with 30/30 pockets and 118/118 industries mapped in
  `taxonomy/industry-crosswalk.json`.

`scripts/apply-top500-taxonomy.ts` merges `taxonomy-additions.json` into the four
taxonomy files. It is idempotent, refuses to redefine an existing id, validates
that every crosswalk target exists and that every industry and pocket is mapped,
and reproduces the sector tree's one-node-per-line format exactly (`+10` lines for
10 nodes, not a whole-file rewrite). `npm run validate:taxonomy` reports
**0 errors, 0 warnings across 741 nodes**.

Two labels needed a row-level decision rather than a mapping:

- **Nestlé under "Banks" — rejected.** The same listing carries Nestlé under Food
  Products and Food Services, so a bank placement contradicts the source's own
  classification of a food manufacturer. The other two labels are imported.
- **Pragma under "Asset Management" — redirected to engineering services.** The
  listing describes an engineering company delivering enterprise asset management;
  the directory label reads as financial asset management and would be wrong.
- **Hyprop under "Investment" — retained as a source, not as a placement.** The
  curated record already carries sourced property placements, so the listing is
  added as an extra source and the generic label does not become a new taxonomy
  link.

## What is imported into the canonical register

`scripts/apply-top500-organizations.ts` reads the export, the category map, the
identity overlay and the existing curated register, and writes
`markets/organizations/organizations.json`. It is idempotent, refuses to overwrite
a curated record it did not create, and refuses to create a second record for an
identity that already exists.

- **196 records** now carry the batch: 208 directory names − 3 folded listings −
  9 already-curated companies = 196.
- **3 listings folded into one record each**, because the source itself shows one
  legal entity:
  - `Barclays Africa Group Limited` → **Absa** (the Barclays page carries Absa's
    contact block: Johannesburg, `barclaysafrica.com`, +27 11 350 4000).
  - `ADCORP` → **Adcorp Holdings** (two profiles, one recruitment group).
  - `ADT Security (Pty) Ltd` → **Fidelity ADT Security** (same Midrand address,
    same telephone number 0860 100 911).
- **9 already-curated companies** gained the listing as an extra source, with no
  change to their sourced placements: VKB, WBHO Construction, Shoprite Holdings,
  RCL Foods, Tiger Brands, Bidfood, Hyprop Investments, Growthpoint Properties and
  Aveng (the directory's "Aveng Africa Limited" is the group the existing record
  already covers).
- **54 dataset-only employer identities** in the contact manifest are now curated
  records. No stub is left beside a curated record for the same company: where the
  contact directory used a shorter name (Telkom, Truworths, Vodacom, Shoprite,
  Microsoft, MultiChoice, ArcelorMittal, Vox Telecom, Eskom Holdings SOC, Harmony
  Gold Mining Company, IBM South Africa, Ogilvy South Africa, Rectron South Africa,
  SAP, Santam Insurance, WSP in Africa, Bowmans (Law Firm), Spoor & Fisher, South
  African Tourism, Clover S.A. Proprietary, Adcorp Group, Airports Company South
  Africa …) that name is carried as an alias on the curated record.
- **1 row rejected** (Nestlé/Banks, above). Rejected rows contribute neither a
  placement nor a source.

Because a directory listing states no headcount or revenue, no record in this batch
carries a `scale` entry either.

Evidence on every record is the directory listing itself
(`type: industry-directory`), the sector label it reports, the location it
reports, and the date it was read. No headcount, revenue, site count or capability
was imported, because a directory listing states none. All 196 records are
`needs-verification`.

### Confidence

Every listing placement is directory evidence, so nothing is `confirmed`:

- **106 records `probable`** — at least one placement lands on a specific
  sub-branch (a level-3 or level-4 taxonomy node, e.g. Fidelity ADT in guarding
  and manned security).
- **90 records `hypothesis`** — every placement lands on a macro-sector node
  because the directory label is generic ("Mining", "Information Technology",
  "Financial Services", "Health"): the source establishes the sector, not the
  sub-branch.
- **0 records unplaced.** All 365 curated companies now resolve to a taxonomy
  node, so nothing falls into the "Not yet classified" branch. Five companies the
  export left uncategorised were placed on their own listing pages instead
  (African Response → market research; Allcare Administrators → scheme and benefit
  administration; Continuity SA → business support services; Premier Verification →
  B-BBEE verification; Trek Bicycle South Africa → branded goods distribution).

## Known limitations

- **Blank locations.** The export leaves `Location(s)` blank on 24 rows, so 17
  records carry no province. They are placed in their branch; the location simply
  is not sourced.
- **Trek Bicycle South Africa.** The URL in the export returns no content. The
  live listing for the same company is cited instead, and the record notes the
  substitution.
- **Directory labels are kept.** Where the directory's label is coarse, the
  placement is coarse and marked `hypothesis` rather than corrected by guesswork
  (for example PricewaterhouseCoopers South Africa arrives under "Financial
  Services" and is placed at the financial-services macro node, and Nestlé keeps
  the "Food Services" label as a second placement).
- **Near-duplicates left as separate organisations.** Where two listings are
  different legal entities, both are kept: Bell Equipment and Bell Equipment
  Company SA, Barloworld and Barloworld Equipment, Fidelity Security Group and
  Fidelity ADT Security, Sasol and Sasol Mining, Discovery Health and Discovery
  Health Medical Scheme, Murray & Roberts and Murray & Roberts Cementation, Avis
  Fleet and Avis Rent a Car & Budget Rent a Car. No parent/subsidiary relationship
  is asserted, because a directory listing does not establish one.
- **34 dataset-only employers that look related to a batch company remain
  separate.** They are divisions, subsidiaries, group parents or unrelated
  companies with a shared word, and merging them would misstate identity. The
  largest are Old Mutual (254 contacts — the group, while the batch lists Old
  Mutual Investment Group), Anglo American (the group, while the batch lists the
  South African arm), Liberty Group South Africa, Tsogo Sun (gaming, while the
  batch lists Tsogo Sun Hotels), HEINEKEN Beverages, ArcelorMittal, PPC Africa,
  Babcock International Group - Africa and internal divisions such as Absa Life,
  Sasol Technology and FirstRand Corporate Centre. `import-report.json` lists all
  34 for review.
